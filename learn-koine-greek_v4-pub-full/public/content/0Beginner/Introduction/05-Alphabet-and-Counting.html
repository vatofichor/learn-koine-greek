Some Further Context

Origins
: Derived from the Phoenician alphabet, with significant innovations including the separation of vowels and consonants.

Logic
: Standardized for Attic Greek; utilized extensively in mathematical logic and physical constants due to distinct glyph separation.

Vowel Distinction
: Eta (H η) and Omega (Ω ω) represent long vowels, distinguishing them from their short counterparts, Epsilon (E ε) and Omicron (O o).

Pronunciation
: Classical Greek had a distinction between long and short vowels, while Koine Greek had a more simplified system.

Consonant Clusters
: Greek has several consonant clusters, such as Xi (Ξ ξ) and Psi (Ψ ψ), which are pronounced as single sounds.

Final Sigma
: The letter Sigma (Σ σ ς) has a special form, ς, which is used only at the end of a word.
