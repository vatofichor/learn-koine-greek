# A Study on Parts of Speech and Tenses

> **Tip:** This study article can be skipped. It serves as an optional reference and practice guide.

    ## Parts of Speech

    | Part of Speech | Definition | Abbreviation |
    |---|---|---|
    | Noun | A word representing a person, place, thing, or idea. | n. |
    | Adjective | A word that modifies or describes a noun or pronoun. | adj. |
    | Adverb | A word that modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb. | adv. |
    | Verbal Adjective | An adjective derived from a verb (e.g., gerundives). | v. adj. |
    | Conjunction | A word used to connect clauses or sentences. | conj. |
    | Pronoun | A word that takes the place of a specific noun. | pro. |
    | Preposition | A word showing the relationship of a noun to another word. | prep. |
    | Reciprocal Pronoun | A pronoun indicating a mutual action or relationship. | recip. pro. |
    | Relative Pronoun | A pronoun that introduces a subordinate/relative clause. | rel. pro. |

    ## Tenses: Time and Aspect

    Tense conveys two primary dimensions of an action: Time (when it occurs) and Aspect (the nature or "type" of the
    action).

    ### Verbal Aspects

    | Aspect | Definition | Abbreviation |
    |---|---|---|
    | Imperfective | Focuses on the process or ongoing nature of the action. | impf. |
    | Aorist | Focuses on the action as a whole or a simple event; "undefined." | aor. |
    | Perfective | Focuses on a completed action with continuing results/state. | perf. |

    ### The Seven Tenses

    | Tense | Definition | Abbreviation |
    |---|---|---|
    | Present | Action occurring in the present, usually imperfective aspect. | pres. |
    | Imperfect | Continuous or repeated action occurring in the past. | imperf. |
    | Future | Action that will occur in the future. | fut. |
    | Aorist | Simple past action or action viewed as a single point in time. | aor. |
    | Perfect | Present state resulting from a completed past action. | perf. |
    | Pluperfect | Past state resulting from an action completed in the remote past. | plup. |
    | Future Perfect | A state that will exist in the future following a completed action. | fut. perf. |

    ## The Tense/Aspect Matrix
    In Koine Greek, Aspect is more fundamental than Time.
    The "Seven Tenses" are effectively coordinates on a grid where one axis is time and the other is the type of action.

    **Aorist** is your "*default*" operator for *narrative facts*.
    **Imperfective** is your "*video*" operator for *ongoing scenes*.
    **Perfective** is your "*static*" operator for *enduring consequences*.

    # Identify verbs in the Septuagint

    Greek verbs are built like modular code: Prefix (Augment) + Root + Tense Marker + Personal Ending.

    ## Primary Endings (Present & Future)
    Used for the Present and Future tenses. These indicate actions in the "now" or "ahead" systems.

    **-ω** 1st Sing. | Present/Future Active: "I [verb]"
    **-εις** 2nd Sing. | Present/Future Active: "You [verb]"
    **-ει** 3rd Sing. | Present/Future Active: "He/She/It [verb]"
    **-ομεν** 1st Plur. | Present/Future Active: "We [verb]"
    **-ετε** 2nd Plur. | Present/Future Active: "You all [verb]"
    **-ουσι(ν)** 3rd Plur. | Present/Future Active: "They [verb]"
    **-σω** Future Marker | The "sigma" (σ) inserted before the ending indicates the Future tense.

    ## Secondary Endings (Imperfect & Aorist)
    Used for the Past systems. These are almost always accompanied by an Augment (an ε- prefix).

    **-ον** 1st Sing. / 3rd Plur. | Imperfect/Aorist Active: "I [verb]" or "They [verb]"
    **-ες** 2nd Sing. | Imperfect/Aorist Active: "You [verb]"
    **-ε(ν)** 3rd Sing. | Imperfect/Aorist Active: "He/She/It [verb]"
    **-ομεν** 1st Plur. | Imperfect/Aorist Active: "We [verb]"
    **-ετε** 2nd Plur. | Imperfect/Aorist Active: "You all [verb]"
    **-σα** Aorist Marker | The "sigma-alpha" (σα) suffix indicates the First Aorist (completed past action).

    ## The "Augment" & Infinitives
    These are the structural "flags" that help you categorize the verb type immediately.

    **ε-** (Prefix) The Augment | A systemic flag placed before the root indicating a Past Tense (Imperfect, Aorist, or
    Pluperfect).
    **-ειν** Present Infinitive | The non-finite form: "To [verb]" (e.g.,*akouein* - to hear).
    **-σαι** Aorist Infinitive | The non-finite form for a completed action: "To [verb]" (viewed as a whole).
    **-ναι** Infinitive Suffix | Used for "mi" verbs (like *tithēmi*) and the Perfect tense.

    When you see a **verb in the Septuagint**, apply *this:* *parsing algorithm:*
    * Check for an **ε**- prefix: If yes, it is a Past Tense (Secondary).
    * Check for a **σ** before the ending: If yes, it is either Future (-σο) or Aorist (-σα).
    * Check the **ending**: Use the tables above to *find the* *Person (Who)* and *Number (How many)*.

    ## Ancient Greek Tense-Aspect Map

    | Aspect Group | Tense | Logical Representation |
    |---|---|---|
    | Perspective (Stative) | Perfect | State_{Now} = f(Action_{Past}) |
    | | Pluperfect | State_{Past} = f(Action_{Pre-Past}) |
    | | Future Perfect | State_{Future} = f(Action_{Future}) |
    | Aorist (Punctiliar) | Aorist | Action \in \{t_{point}\} |
    | Imperfective (Linear) | Present | Action \in [t_{start}, t_{now}] |
    | | Imperfect | Action \in [t_{start}, t_{end}] \subset Past |

    ### Logic & Functional Definition
    **1. Perspective (Stative Aspect)**
    Focuses on the existing state resulting from a completed action. It is not just that the action is "done," but that
    the "doneness" defines the current subject.
    * Perfect: Present state resulting from past action (e.g., "I have seen" = "I am in a state of knowing").
    * Pluperfect: Past state resulting from a previous action (e.g., "I had seen").
    * Future Perfect: A state that will exist in the future (e.g., "I will have seen").

    **2. Aorist (Aorist Aspect)**
    Derived from a-oristos ("without boundaries"). It treats the action as a single point or "snapshot."
    * Aorist: The "simple past." It ignores duration or internal complexity. It simply states that the event occurred.

    **3. Imperfective (Continuous Aspect)**
    Focuses on the process, duration, or repetition of the action.
    * Present: Action happening now, viewed as an ongoing process.
    * Imperfect: Action happening in the past, viewed as an ongoing process (e.g., "I was writing" vs. the aorist "I
    wrote").

    ### Visualizing the Timeline
    * Imperfective: A line indicating duration.
    * Aorist: A dot indicating a point.
    * Perspective: A vector indicating an action leading to a fixed state.
