    Augustine of Hippo

    Augustine of Hippo (354–430) was a staunch defender of the Septuagint (LXX), viewing it as divinely inspired, not
    just a translation. Despite Jerome favoring Hebrew, Augustine prioritized the LXX for its authority in Western
    tradition and unity, arguing that disparities were prophetic enhancements. In his correspondence with Jerome, he
    feared that deviating from the LXX would damage the Church’s authority. He upheld the "Septuaginta auctoritas"
    (authority of the seventy), and his canonization of the OT included deuterocanonical books found in the Greek
    version. His approach combined theological belief in its inspiration with practical use in pastoral teaching.

    Lucianic "
    Lucianic refers to the style of 2nd-century Greek satirist Lucian of Samosata (witty, ironical) or, in theology, the
    **3rd/4th-century Lucian of Antioch's revision** of the Greek Bible.

    It characterizes a smoother, more readable text, forming the **basis for the Byzantine text-type and Textus
    Receptus**.

    **The Lucianic Text (Antiochian):**
    A revision of the Septuagint (Greek Old Testament) by Lucian of Antioch, noted for improving Greek style, removing
    obscurities, and creating a smoother, more readable text.

    Notes

    Lucian of Antioch (c. 240–312) was an influential Christian **theologian, presbyter, and martyr known for producing
    a critical, widely used revision of the Greek Bible (Septuagint/New Testament).** As a key figure in the School of
    Antioch, he emphasized literal-historical interpretation over allegorical methods. His teachings on Christology are
    considered a forerunner to Arianism, as he taught that the Word was a created being. He died in Nicomedia during the
    persecution of Maximinus Daia.

    **Bible Revision:** Created the "Lucianic" text, which influenced the Byzantine text-type.

    **Theology:** Championed literal biblical interpretation, opposing Alexandrian allegory.

    **Legacy:** Regarded as a precursor to Arius; his students included key Arian leaders.

    **Martyrdom:** Executed in 312 AD, honored as a saint.
